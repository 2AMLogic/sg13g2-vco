## Method

A phase-noise number without its method is not a result (`CLAUDE.md`),
so the whole method is restated here rather than cited.

- **Why not a spectrum.** ngspice ships neither PSS nor pnoise, and its
  transient analysis runs with device noise sources switched off. A
  periodogram of this deck's own transient -- or a period-jitter
  extraction converted to `L(df)` -- would therefore measure the
  solver's truncation error, not the circuit's phase noise, at any
  transient length. See `README.md`, "Why not a periodogram".
- **Estimator**: the impulse sensitivity function, measured. A
  triangular charge impulse of half-width 2e-12 s is injected at
  each of 10 carrier phases, and the PERMANENT excess-phase
  step it produces is read from the shift in the differential tank
  voltage's crossing times. Reduced through
  `Gq(phi) = dphi/dq` [rad/C] and
  `L(df) = Gq_rms^2 * S_i / (2 * (2*pi*df)^2)`.
- **q_max is never measured**, deliberately: the unnormalised
  `Gq = Gamma/q_max` is what the impulse response gives directly, and
  `q_max` cancels out of the kernel. A `q_max` for a tank loaded by
  32 varactor instances, two EM-fitted inductor ladders and a MIM would
  have carried an error bar larger than the ISF's own.
- **The kernel's factor of 2 is named, not assumed.** The form above
  counts noise folding down from BOTH `w0+dw` and `w0-dw`;
  Hajimiri & Lee 1998 eq. 14 as literally written counts only the
  upper sideband and is 3 dB lower. The form used here is the one that
  reproduces Leeson's linear-tank kernel exactly for an ideal LC, which
  `run_method_check.sh` verifies numerically (KA-4) -- and it is the
  more pessimistic of the two. Both are printed by `pn_l_dbc`.
- **Transient**: `tran 1p 9.257800891530e-09`, timestep ceiling
  `2p`, startup from the 10 mV differential
  `.ic` (`v(OUTP)=3.305`, `v(OUTN)=3.295`).
- **Discarded startup window**: 0 .. 2.5e-9 s. Crossings
  before it are not compared. The value is
  `sim/oscillator-core`'s, derived there from a MEASURED
  90 %-envelope settling time of 0.487 .. 1.256 ns across its pilot
  corners, i.e. 2.0x the slowest observed.
- **Impulse train**: first impulse at 3.5e-9 s, spacing
  5.757800891530e-10 s = (3 + 1/10) periods at the
  declared nominal 5.384e9 Hz. The fractional part is what walks
  the carrier phase so one run samples the whole ISF; the integer part
  is what lets the amplitude transient decay between impulses. The
  measured carrier came out 0.0046 % from that nominal, and
  the resulting phase grid's largest gap is recorded per realisation.
- **Plateau**: each step is read over the last 0.35 of
  the inter-impulse interval, so the amplitude transient is excluded by
  construction; the plateau's own ripple is recorded per impulse.
- **The reference run is this same deck with zero-amplitude impulses**,
  so both decks carry an identical PWL breakpoint set and take
  identical timesteps until the first non-zero impulse. The
  pre-impulse crossing-offset residual is therefore an exact null, and
  it is recorded per realisation as `null_floor_s` -- this method's
  own noise floor, measured rather than asserted.
- **Crossing threshold**: ONE value, the reference run's trapezoidal
  time average over the measurement window, applied to both runs. A
  per-run mean would have folded any DC shift the perturbation caused
  into the very crossing times being measured.
- **Port noise** `S_i`: ngspice small-signal `noise`, unit AC
  current probe across the port, port voltage as the output, so
  `inoise_spectrum` is the port's Norton-equivalent noise current
  density. Swept over 3e9 .. 9e9 Hz
  (7 points), median taken, band spread recorded.
- **Estimator validation**: `run_method_check.sh`, the same
  `pn_crossings`/`pn_isf_steps`/`pn_gamma_stats`/`pn_l_dbc`
  against synthetic oscillators with closed-form ISF and closed-form
  phase noise, with derived tolerances.

  2.000000e-12 s).
- **Equivalent port noise**: tank 2.64526811e-23 A^2/Hz
  (5.14321700e-12 A/sqrt(Hz), 9.296 % peak-to-peak across
  the swept band); tail 5.98291421e-23 A^2/Hz (7.73493000e-12 A/sqrt(Hz),
  56.652 %).
- **ISF at the differential tank port**: `Gq_rms` =
  1.329958e+13 +- 4.596977e+11 rad/C over 4 realisations.
- **`L(1 MHz)` = -102.2763 +- 0.2953 dBc/Hz**
  (realisations spanned -102.4447 .. -101.8343 dBc/Hz).
  Row-4 bounds -105 target / -115
  stretch: **TARGET NOT MET** at this corner.
- **`L(10 MHz)` = -122.2763 +- 0.2953 dBc/Hz**. Row-4 companion
  bounds -125 / -135: **TARGET NOT MET** at
  this corner. This offset is reported from the same 1/f^2 kernel as
  the 1 MHz number, so it is 20 dB below it by construction and is NOT
  independent evidence -- see the limits below.
- **Variance across realisations**: the ensemble is
  4e-15 -4e-15 8e-15 -8e-15 C of injected charge -- both signs (which cancels
  any even-order term in the phase response) at two magnitudes (which
  is the linearity test the first-order ISF assumption stands on). The
  `Gq_rms` spread between the two magnitudes was
  3.746 %. **This is not a Monte Carlo variance**: this
  estimator has no random input. See `README.md`, "What the variance
  here is, and what it is not".
- **Non-PDK inductor model, propagated** (`sim/README.md` rule 6):
  `sim/inductor-model/sg13g2_inductor_em.spice` sha256
  `6f24f9857ab7810c91bda5f6307e78f1dfcbe2afd750bb023e28156ded06d6a0`. The PDK ships NO spiral-inductor
  ngspice model (`sim/pdk.json` `known_model_gaps.spiral_inductor`),
  so this number is not a PDK-model result. `design/vco.sch`
  instantiates the `p11` geometry, one of the three the EM extraction
  actually covered, so its own bars apply rather than an extrapolation:
  a +-10 % bar on the EM-measured Q propagates to at
  most +-0.414 dB on `L` (through the tank loss noise), and
  the 0.90 % rms lumped-fit residual to at most
  +-0.078 dB (through the resonating C in `q_max`);
  **+-0.421 dB in quadrature**. Two of that model's stated
  limits are NOT quantifiable as a dB bar and do not appear in it: the
  EM solve was run at one process point and one temperature, and no
  measured silicon backs any of it.

## What this does NOT show

- **The noise is taken at the DC operating point, not over the cycle.**
  ngspice's `noise` is a small-signal analysis about the oscillator's
  UNSTABLE EQUILIBRIUM, where each device of the cross-coupled pair
  carries half the tail current continuously. The running oscillator's
  noise sources are cyclostationary. This is the single largest stated
  limit on the number above, it is not corrected here, and its sign
  depends on how hard the pair switches.
- **All circuit noise is referred to the tank port.** The primary
  number weights the whole Norton-equivalent noise by the TANK port's
  ISF. Noise physically originating in the tail branch should be
  weighted by the tail port's ISF instead, which measured
  -10.09 dB different (`Gq_rms` tail 4.16277044e+12 vs tank
  1.329958e+13 rad/C); referring the tail port's own noise through its
  own ISF gives -108.8171 dBc/Hz. That is an indicator of how much the
  assumption could matter, not a correction and not a rigorous bound.
- **Only the 1/f^2 region.** The kernel used is the white-noise one.
  The 1/f^3 corner -- which is set by the ISF's DC coefficient c0
  acting on device flicker noise -- is not evaluated, so neither offset
  above is evidence about close-in noise, and the 10 MHz number is 20 dB
  below the 1 MHz one by construction rather than by measurement.
- **No AM-to-PM conversion**, no supply or substrate noise path, and no
  noise from anything outside the netlist.
- **One corner**, as stated above. Whether the tank's loss, the pair's
  noise or the varactor bank's Q move this number at
  `mos_ss`/`cap_wcs`/`hbt_wcs` or at -40/+125 C is unmeasured.
